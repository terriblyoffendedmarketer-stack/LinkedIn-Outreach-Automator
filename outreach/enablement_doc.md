# Storylane ambassador — what we need from you

**One LinkedIn post. That's it.**

Not a product review. Not a feature walkthrough. Just your honest take on what interactive demos have done for you — the way you'd tell a peer over coffee.

---

## What to write about

You know how you use Storylane better than anyone. Pick whichever of these feels most true to your experience:

- **Website** — you put a demo on your site and something changed (traffic, conversions, fewer "can I see the product first?" emails)
- **Sales calls** — you stopped relying on a live environment and something got easier
- **Post-call follow-up** — you started sending a demo link after meetings and noticed a difference in how deals moved
- **Pre-call warmup** — you sent a demo before the first call and the conversation started differently
- **Personalization** — you built demos for different ICPs or accounts and it changed how you pitch
- **Speed** — something that used to take a week now takes an afternoon

One of those is probably your story. You don't need all of them — just the one that's real.

---

## How to turn your raw thoughts into a post

You don't need to write from scratch. Just dump your unfiltered thoughts into your AI tool of choice (ChatGPT, Claude, whatever you use) along with this prompt:

---

**Paste this prompt + your raw notes:**

```
Write a LinkedIn post based on these notes from me: [paste your thoughts here — messy is fine]

Rules:
- Hook in the first two lines: a tension, a contrast, or something that makes someone stop scrolling. Don't start with "I".
- Sound like how I actually talk, not polished corporate writing
- 150–200 words max
- No bullet points or numbered lists
- Storylane is the tool I used, not the hero — the hero is the outcome or the insight
- End with one punchy line that lands the point
- Avoid: "game-changer", "in today's world", "I'm excited to share", "it's been a journey"
```

---

That's all it takes. Edit the draft to sound more like you, add one specific detail from your actual experience, and you're done.

---

## The goal

The best posts from Storylane creators aren't about Storylane — they're about a problem that got easier, a workflow that changed, a result that surprised them. Storylane just happens to be in the story. That's what makes people pay attention.

You've already done the hard part. You've used it, you've seen what it does. This is just putting that into words.

# Storylane Creator Guide - to write authoritative LinkedIn posts

## **You already have the insight. Now let’s find the hook.**

You've used Storylane. 

You've seen something work, something break, or something surprise you. **That's your post.** The only job now is *to find the right hook for it.*

**Step 1: Name your insight**

What's the one thing you noticed? Write it out plainly, no pressure. Richard's was: "People are sitting on demo engagement data that could tell them exactly what's working — and nobody's reading it."

**Step 2: Find the problem inside it**

What's broken for someone who hasn't figured this out yet? That's your hook. Richard's problem: "Most teams are counting demo requests. The actual signal — what buyers did inside the demo — is invisible."

**Step 3: Write the hook**

Your hook is a promise. The post is the delivery. **LinkedIn only shows the first two lines before the "read more" cutoff — so those two lines need to make someone feel like they're about to miss something.**

---

## **Some Hook examples and why they work:**

- ***"Most teams are still counting demo requests. They have no idea what they're missing."*** — Names the mistake without being preachy. The second line creates stakes. You want to know what you're missing.
- ***"Before 'signals' was a hot topic, we stumbled into one.
Our sales team absolutely hated it."*** 
— Conflict and curiosity in two lines. You need to know what happened next.
- ***"You have intent data sitting in plain sight, and it's not in your CRM."*** — Clean contrarian framing. Anyone who cares about pipeline reads this and wants to know why they're wrong.
- ***"Storylane's HubSpot integration changed how we track demo engagement."** 
— This one is interesting, as on paper it leads with the product, which isn’t the best way to set the premise, but t*he real subject it’s actually leading is “demo engagement”. and  2nd subject is ‘HubSpot’, which is a pretty universal tool. And it closes it with a *shift*, so it’s still a hook that delivers a complete promise in one line.
- ***“Your demo knows who's going to buy. You're just not listening to it.”***

#### **Hook example based on personal experience.**

(These work too, but totally depend on the strength of personal brand/ audience.)

- "I want to show you a really cool experiment you can run." - Personal angles.
- "We've been using Storylane for a while now and wanted to share some thoughts." - like a personal product review.

The main idea is that the **first two lines make a promise that the post has to deliver on.**

### Seemingly good hook that might not be the best:

- ***"Demo engagement is the most honest signal in your pipeline. Nobody's reading it.”***
— Though it's a bit contrarian, but it's a complete take. If this entire line were a single post, it tells me everything I know that the post is going to be about, so there is no tease factor.

## **The rest of the post**

Once the hook is right, just deliver on it:

- What was broken or invisible
- What you did or noticed (your real experience — this is your authority)
- What Storylane made possible that nothing else did
- One closing line that sticks

**The closing line should feel like the payoff** for everything above it. "Start counting demo behavior. That's where the buyers are hiding." earns that spot because the whole post built to it.

.

**Your hook is a promise. Your post is the delivery.**
